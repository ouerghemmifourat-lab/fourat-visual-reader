# Charts: bars, lines, pies, scatter

General: lead with the trend or ranking in plain words (level 3), then the supporting figures (level 2), keep the
construction (axes, colours, encodings) to one short sentence (level 1). Values come from the data table or spec; from an
image, say "approximately" and read only labelled values.

## Bar chart (simple, grouped, stacked)

Announce: "A bar chart of {measure} by {category}, {N} bars[, {S} series: …], in {unit}."
Message: the ranking or the gap: "France is highest, Spain close behind, Portugal far lower."
Key figures: highest with value, lowest with value, the ratio or difference in words ("almost double", "a third less").
Detail:
- ≤ 6 bars: all, in chart order, groups of 3.
- More: top 3, bottom 3, then the rest on request; or in chart order if the order carries meaning (months, ages).
- Grouped: by category, each series value ("France: online 12, stores 30"); or by series on request.
- Stacked: per bar, the total first, then the parts ("France, total 42: online 12, stores 30").
Negative bars: "minus 3", and say that values go below zero in the announce.

## Line chart (time series)

Announce: "A line chart of {measure} from {start} to {end}, one point per {step}, {S} series, in {unit}."
Message: the trend in everyday words with endpoints: "Revenue rises steadily from 10 to 25 million, with a dip in 2020."
Key figures: first value, last value, peak with its date, trough with its date; for several series, the relationship
("A stays above B; the gap narrows after 2021").
Detail: by segment ("2015 to 2018: rising from 10 to 16"), never point by point beyond 8 points unless asked. For a
specific date: the value, and the neighbours if asked.
Vocabulary: rises / falls / is flat / peaks / dips / recovers / levels off / fluctuates. Percent change is a useful
summary ("up about 150 percent over the period"); say "percentage points" for differences between rates.

## Pie and donut

Announce: "A pie chart of {whole} in {N} parts."
Message: the dominant share and whether it is over half: "Rent takes more than half."
Detail: shares from largest to smallest, in percent as printed; ≤ 6 parts all, else the 4 largest and "the other {N−4}
total {X} percent". Do not invent decimals; if parts do not sum to 100, say "they sum to 98 percent as printed".

## Scatter plot

Announce: "A scatter plot of {y} against {x}, {N} points, in {units}."
Message: the relationship in words: "higher income goes with higher life expectancy, with a few exceptions."
Key figures: named extremes if points are labelled; clusters or outliers by name.
Detail: labelled points on request. Give a correlation coefficient only if asked and only if it is in the source.

## Histogram and box plot

Histogram: range, the most frequent bin, the shape (symmetric, skewed, two humps). Box plot: median, the quartile box,
whiskers, outliers; one number per sentence.

## Multiple charts (small multiples, dashboards)

Announce how many charts and their common structure, then the one overall message, then one line per chart; detail per
chart on request.

## Axis and scale warnings

Say it when it changes the meaning: a truncated axis ("the axis starts at 80, so differences look larger than they
are"), a log scale, a dual axis, a break in the series.
