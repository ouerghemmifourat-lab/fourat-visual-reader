# Speaking numbers, units, dates and symbols — English

Write everything as words before TTS. `say-as` differs by engine and must not be relied on for correctness.

## Cardinals

- 0 zero; 1,234 one thousand two hundred thirty-four (no "and" in US style; "and" is fine in UK style, be consistent).
- Large numbers: thousand, million, billion (10⁹), trillion (10¹²). Say "about 2.1 million", not "2,100,000".
- Decimals: digits one by one after "point": 3.25 "three point two five"; 0.5 "zero point five" (or "a half" when the
  context is a fraction). In summaries, at most two decimals; say "about" when you round.
- Negative: "minus three". Never "dash three" or "negative" unless the field convention requires it.
- Thousands separator is a comma, decimal is a point; "1,5" written by a French author means one and a half.
- Ranges: "from 10 to 15", "between 10 and 15". Never read the dash.
- Approximations: "about", "roughly", "just under", "a little over".

## Ordinals and ranks

first, second, third, fourth…; "ranked third"; "the 21st" → "the twenty-first".

## Percentages and rates

- 12 % → "twelve percent"; 12.5 % → "twelve point five percent".
- A difference between two rates is in "percentage points": 12 % to 15 % is "up three percentage points", not
  "three percent".
- Per mille, basis points: say the words.

## Currency

- € 12 → "twelve euros"; $3.50 → "three dollars fifty" or "three fifty"; £ 1.2 million → "one point two million pounds".
- ISO codes: EUR euros, USD US dollars, GBP pounds, CHF Swiss francs, JPY yen. Cents only when they matter.

## Units

Always the full word; plural with the number, singular only for exactly one: "one kilometre", "one point five
kilometres".

## Years, dates, times, durations

- Years: 2024 "twenty twenty-four"; 1998 "nineteen ninety-eight"; 2005 "two thousand five". A year is not a quantity.
- Dates: say the order explicitly: 03/03/2024 → "March third, twenty twenty-four" (or "the third of March"). Quarters:
  "Q3 2024" → "the third quarter of twenty twenty-four". Months abbreviated (Jan.) → full word.
- Times: 14:30 → "two thirty p m" or "fourteen thirty" (24-hour contexts). 09:05 → "nine oh five".
- Durations: 1 h 20 → "one hour twenty minutes"; 90 s → "ninety seconds"; 2:15 (mm:ss) → "two minutes fifteen seconds".

## Phone numbers, codes, IDs

Groups of three or four digits with a pause: "five five five, one two three, four five six seven". Repeated digits:
"double five" is British, "five five" is American; pick one. Letters in IDs are spelled; "0" is "zero" (or "oh" in
phone numbers, US).

## Acronyms and abbreviations

- Pronounceable and well known (NASA, NATO): say as a word.
- Otherwise spell the letters, and give the expansion once if you know it: "GDP, gross domestic product".
- Abbreviations in tables: expand (avg → average, max → maximum, min → minimum, no. → number, approx. → about,
  vs → versus, etc. → and so on, e.g. → for example, N/A → not available, YoY → year on year, Q → quarter).

## Fractions and simple formulas

½ "a half", ⅓ "a third", ¾ "three quarters", 2/3 "two thirds", 5/8 "five eighths".
x² "x squared", x³ "x cubed", xⁿ "x to the n", (a+b)/2 "a plus b, over two", a·b "a times b", a_i "a sub i".
For anything longer, follow ClearSpeak style (MathCAT, MIT licence): natural phrasing, "the fraction … over …" only when
needed to avoid ambiguity.

## Chemistry (practice, no authoritative source)

H₂O "H two O", CO₂ "C O two", NaCl "N a C l" — or the name when known: "water", "carbon dioxide", "sodium chloride".
